Questions for Dad — Symptom Clarification Worksheet

Fill in his answers as you talk through this together. There's no rush — it's fine to do it over a couple of sittings.

How to open the conversation:

"Dad, we're not asking this to figure out if it's real — we already know it's real and it's bad. Doctors work like detectives, and small details, like whether your scalp is tender or what color your urine is, can point them straight to the right tests. Can I ask you some specific questions and write down your answers?"

Before you start: if possible, have a thermometer nearby for next time so an actual temperature can be logged during a flare — "I felt warm" is harder to act on than "100.4°F."

1. Safety Check (ask these first)

These flag anything urgent — if any answer is yes, mention it to a doctor right away rather than waiting.

Has your vision ever blurred, doubled, dimmed, or gone dark — even briefly — during an episode?
Even seconds of vision change alongside head pain is a warning sign at his age.
Does your jaw or tongue ache, cramp, or get tired specifically while chewing?
Pain that's brought on by chewing (not just present all the time) points to reduced blood flow in facial arteries.
Is your scalp tender or sore to the touch — does it hurt to comb your hair or rest your head on a pillow?
Have you ever seen blood, or a dark tarry look, in your stool, or coughed up blood when your chest hurts?

2. Physical Signs You Can See or Measure

These are objective and hard to dismiss as "in his head" — they're the strongest evidence against the hypochondria label.

Do you get chills or night sweats during one of these 48-hour flares?
Any skin changes during an attack — hives, flushing/redness, or small red or purple pinprick-sized spots (especially on the lower legs)?
Small purple spots on the legs can be a visible sign of blood vessel inflammation.
What color is your urine when your stomach or back/flank pain flares up? (normal / dark or tea-colored / other)
Dark urine during a flare can point to kidney involvement.
Have your joints — wrists, knuckles, knees — ever looked swollen, felt warm, or looked red during a flare?
Have you lost weight without trying over the last 6 months?

3. What the Pain Actually Feels Like

The exact character and movement of the pain helps point toward a nerve, vascular, or inflammatory cause.

How would you describe the sensation? (throbbing/pulsing • burning, shooting, or electric • a deep bruised ache • sharp/stabbing • other)
When it moves, does the first spot stop hurting, or does it keep hurting while a new spot starts too?
Does it follow the same path every time (e.g., always face → stomach → back), or does the order change episode to episode?
Is it on one side of your head/body, or both?
When your "lungs" hurt, does it get sharply worse with a deep breath in, coughing, or sneezing?
Pain that worsens with breathing usually comes from the chest wall/lining, not the lungs themselves — useful to describe exactly this way to a doctor.

4. How He Feels Between Episodes

This helps separate a constant underlying condition from one that comes and goes in true attacks.

Between episodes, do you feel completely back to normal, or is there leftover fatigue, stiffness, or soreness?
In the morning, do your shoulders, neck, or hips feel stiff for more than about 45 minutes before loosening up?
Prolonged morning stiffness in these areas is a classic sign worth mentioning specifically.
How often do the flares happen — roughly once a month, every couple of weeks, or unpredictable?

5. Timeline, Triggers & Medications

Pinpointing when this started and what surrounds it can point toward (or rule out) an external trigger.

When did the very first episode happen? Had you started any new medication, eye drops, or supplement around that time?
Did the flares start shortly after any illness — a cold, flu, COVID-19, stomach bug, or a tick/insect bite?
Does anything seem to bring on a flare — certain foods, alcohol, cold or hot weather, poor sleep, stress, or physical exertion?
Does anything reliably help during a flare — rest, heat, a specific position, an OTC medication?
If you take Tylenol, Advil/ibuprofen, or an antihistamine during a flare, does it help at all, or does it do nothing?
Has any family member ever had similar unexplained pain episodes, an autoimmune disease, or an undiagnosed illness?
Do you have any Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, Armenian, Turkish, or Jewish ancestry?
Have you ever had an unusually severe reaction to an insect sting (bee, wasp, etc.)?
Once filled out, keep this alongside the 4-week symptom log — together they give the doctor a much fuller picture than describing the pain from memory in the exam room.
