A note before we go

It began as a voice note at Starbucks.

I recorded this trip as it happened, speaking into my phone in the quiet pockets of time between flights, castle courtyards, narrow coastal roads, and pub suppers. These pages smooth out the rough edges of those quick voice memos and fill in the historical and geographical texture of the places we passed through—while keeping the exact point of view that made the notes mine.

My parents joined me for the Ireland journey, with Halifax serving as the anchor at both ends of our Atlantic crossing. What follows is not a checklist of everything a tourist ought to see, but the trip as we actually lived it: the ancient stones, the unexpected detours, the meals we rated at the table, and the occasional hotel lift that had other plans.

Afterword · the way home

The places between the places.

I went looking for castles, and I found plenty—from polished Anglo-Norman earldoms to vine-choked towers crumbling beside tidal inlets. Yet the memories that keep returning are rarely the famous postcard angles. They are Mom and me walking five kilometres through Dublin after dark, Dad’s running commentary on the evening meal, Rosie the horse pulling our trap steadily up through the Gap of Dunloe, and the sudden decision to pull the car over because a cliff or an empty beach demanded a closer look.

I loved the sprawling grounds and woodland water features at Blarney more than I ever expected. I loved the quiet glide of an open boat across Killarney’s three lakes. I loved the way a twelve-hour day that already felt full to the brim could still surprise us with one more medieval ruin at dusk. Even the less glamorous details—a missing wallet at the gate, a temperamental hotel lift, a tiny room above a wedding reception, or sorting laundry at an Airbnb—belong in the record. That is what travelling together actually felt like.

By the time I was flying back west over northern Ontario toward Winnipeg, I had a phone full of voice notes and a head full of history. More importantly, I had shared the open road with Mom and Dad. I would do that part again in a heartbeat.

Brent
